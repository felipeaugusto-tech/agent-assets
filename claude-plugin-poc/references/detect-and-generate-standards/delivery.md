## Purpose

Establish consistent delivery practices that enable predictable, sustainable, and transparent software delivery. These standards cover sprint mechanics, estimation, ceremonies, and capacity management to keep the team aligned and productive.

## Audience

Scrum masters, engineering managers, tech leads, and all team members participating in agile delivery.

## Standards

### Sprint Conventions

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Sprint duration is fixed and consistent (do not change mid-project without team agreement) | Predictability for planning and stakeholder expectations | Team agreement document |
| 2 | Sprint has a clear goal stated at the start of each sprint | Focuses the team on outcomes, not just tasks | Sprint planning output |
| 3 | No new work is added mid-sprint without the team's consent and a scope trade-off | Protects sprint commitments and team focus | Scrum master enforcement |

### Estimation

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Use a consistent estimation method across the team (story points, T-shirt sizing, or time-based) | Enables velocity tracking and capacity planning | Sprint planning process |
| 2 | The development team estimates -- not managers or product owners | People doing the work are best positioned to assess complexity | Sprint planning observation |
| 3 | Estimates include testing, code review, and deployment time -- not just coding | Prevents underestimation of total effort | Estimation guidelines |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Use Planning Poker or similar technique to surface disagreements | Reveals different assumptions about scope | Sprint planning |
| 2 | Maintain a reference story for each point value | Calibrates estimates across the team | Estimation sessions |
| 3 | Re-estimate stories that are significantly older than 2 sprints | Context and codebase change over time | Backlog grooming |

#### MAY (Optional Best Practices)

| # | Standard | Benefit | Trade-off |
|---|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Track estimation accuracy over time (estimated vs actual) | Improves future estimates | Requires tracking discipline |
| 2 | Use #NoEstimates for well-understood, similarly-sized work | Reduces ceremony time | Only works with mature, consistent teams |

### Sprint Planning

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Only pull stories that meet the Definition of Ready into the sprint | Prevents mid-sprint discovery of missing requirements | Sprint planning gate |
| 2 | Commit to a sprint goal, not just a list of stories | Gives the team flexibility to adjust scope while preserving the outcome | Sprint planning output |
| 3 | Do not exceed team capacity (based on historical velocity) | Prevents burnout and missed commitments | Velocity comparison |
| 4 | Time-box sprint planning (2 hours for a 2-week sprint) | Prevents analysis paralysis | Facilitator enforcement |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Break stories into tasks during planning | Surfaces hidden complexity early | Stories estimated at 5+ points |
| 2 | Identify dependencies between stories and plan ordering | Prevents blocked work mid-sprint | When stories have dependencies |
| 3 | Account for planned time off, holidays, and meetings | Realistic capacity | Every sprint |

### Daily Standups

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Time-box to 15 minutes | Respects everyone's time | Facilitator enforcement |
| 2 | Every team member answers: What did I do? What will I do? Any blockers? | Surfaces progress and blockers quickly | Standup observation |
| 3 | Blockers are captured and assigned an owner for resolution | Prevents blockers from lingering | Blocker tracking |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Hold standups at the same time every day | Builds team rhythm | All teams |
| 2 | Walk the board (review tickets in progress) instead of going person-by-person | Focuses on flow and bottlenecks | When using a visual board |
| 3 | Take detailed discussions offline | Keeps standup focused | When a topic exceeds 2 minutes |

#### MAY (Optional Best Practices)

| # | Standard | Benefit | Trade-off |
|---|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Use async standups (Slack/Teams bot) for distributed teams | Accommodates time zones | Loses real-time discussion |
| 2 | Skip standups when the entire team is pairing/mobbing | Redundant when communication is continuous | Requires team discipline |

### Retrospectives

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Hold a retrospective at the end of every sprint | Continuous improvement requires regular reflection | Calendar, meeting notes |
| 2 | Generate at least one actionable improvement item per retro | Retros without actions are wasted time | Action item tracking |
| 3 | Review action items from the previous retro at the start | Ensures follow-through | Retro agenda |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Rotate retro formats to keep engagement high | Prevents retro fatigue | Every few sprints |
| 2 | Time-box to 60 minutes for a 2-week sprint | Prevents diminishing returns | All retros |
| 3 | Create a safe space -- no blame, focus on systems and processes | Encourages honest feedback | Always |

#### MAY (Optional Best Practices)

| # | Standard | Benefit | Trade-off |
|---|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Use anonymous input collection before discussion | Surfaces sensitive topics | Less spontaneous discussion |
| 2 | Invite stakeholders to occasional retros | Broader perspective | May inhibit candid feedback |

### Demo / Sprint Review

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Demo completed work to stakeholders at the end of each sprint | Validates that the work meets expectations | Sprint review attendance |
| 2 | Only demo work that meets the Definition of Done | Prevents demoing incomplete or untested work | DoD checklist |
| 3 | Collect feedback and capture it as backlog items | Closes the feedback loop | Backlog entries |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Use a live environment (staging), not slides or screenshots | More credible and interactive | When staging is available |
| 2 | Time-box to 30-60 minutes | Respects stakeholder time | All reviews |
| 3 | Let the developer who built the feature demo it | They can answer questions most effectively | When possible |

### Escalation Procedures

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Blockers unresolved for more than 24 hours are escalated to the scrum master or engineering manager | Prevents long-lived blockers from derailing the sprint | Blocker tracking |
| 2 | Sprint goal at risk triggers a conversation with the product owner about scope trade-offs | Protects team morale and stakeholder trust | Scrum master monitoring |
| 3 | Production incidents follow the incident response process (see operations standards) | Production issues have their own severity-based process | Incident runbook |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|

## Review Cadence

Review these standards quarterly or when:
- Team composition changes significantly (new members, departures)
- Velocity trends indicate a systemic issue (consistently under/over committing)
- A retrospective action item targets a delivery process improvement
- The organization changes its delivery methodology

Reviewer: Scrum master or engineering manager, with input from the team.
