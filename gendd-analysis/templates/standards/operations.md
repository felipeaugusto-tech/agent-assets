## Purpose

Define operational standards that ensure system reliability, rapid incident response, and sustainable on-call practices. These standards cover monitoring, alerting, incident management, deployments, and disaster recovery -- the practices that keep production systems healthy.

## Audience

DevOps engineers, SREs, on-call engineers, engineering managers, and developers who deploy and operate production systems.

## Standards

### Monitoring & Alerting

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Monitor the four golden signals: latency, traffic, errors, and saturation | Industry-standard indicators of system health | Monitoring dashboard review |
| 2 | Every production service has a health check endpoint | Enables automated liveness and readiness probes | Infrastructure config |
| 3 | Alerts fire only on actionable conditions -- every alert requires a defined response | Alert fatigue leads to missed real incidents | Alert runbook mapping |
| 4 | Critical alerts reach on-call within 5 minutes via paging (not email or chat only) | Time-sensitive issues need immediate attention | Paging system config |
| 5 | Dashboards exist for all production services showing real-time health | Single pane of glass for operational awareness | Dashboard review |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Use anomaly detection in addition to static thresholds | Catches novel failure modes | Mature monitoring setup |
| 2 | Monitor business metrics alongside technical metrics | Technical health does not always reflect business health | Revenue-critical services |
| 3 | Set up synthetic monitoring (external probes) for customer-facing endpoints | Detects issues from the user's perspective | Public-facing services |
| 4 | Review and tune alert thresholds quarterly | Prevents alert drift as traffic and behavior evolve | All alerting rules |

#### MAY (Optional Best Practices)

| # | Standard | Benefit | Trade-off |
|---|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Implement distributed tracing across services | End-to-end request visibility | Instrumentation overhead |
| 2 | Use SLO-based alerting (error budget burn rate) | Reduces alert noise, focuses on user impact | Requires SLO definition |

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|

| 3 | Publish postmortems to the engineering team | Shared learning across teams | P1/P2 incidents |

**Incident Severity Levels:**

| Severity | Description | Response Time | Update Cadence | Examples |
|----------|-------------|---------------|----------------|---------|
| P1 - Critical | Service down, data loss, security breach | 15 minutes | Every 30 min | Full outage, data corruption, active exploit |
| P2 - High | Major feature degraded, significant user impact | 1 hour | Every 1 hour | Partial outage, major performance degradation |
| P3 - Medium | Minor feature degraded, workaround exists | 4 hours | Daily | Intermittent errors, non-critical feature down |
| P4 - Low | Cosmetic issue, no user impact | Next business day | As resolved | UI glitch, non-blocking bug |

**Escalation Path:**

| Duration | Action |
|----------|--------|

### Runbooks

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Every alert has a linked runbook | On-call engineers need to know what to do | Alert-to-runbook mapping |
| 2 | Runbooks include: symptoms, diagnosis steps, resolution steps, escalation criteria | Complete information reduces MTTR | Runbook review |
| 3 | Runbooks are tested and updated after every incident that reveals gaps | Stale runbooks are dangerous | Postmortem action items |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Include rollback procedures in every deployment-related runbook | Fast rollback is the safest resolution for many incidents | Deployment runbooks |
| 2 | Include "safe to wait until business hours" guidance for non-critical alerts | Prevents unnecessary 3am wake-ups | P3/P4 alerts |

**Runbook Template:**

### Deployment Standards

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Every deployment has a defined rollback procedure that can be executed within {ROLLBACK_TIME} | Fast rollback is the safest mitigation for bad deployments | Deployment runbook |
| 2 | Run smoke tests after every production deployment | Validates basic functionality in the real environment | CI/CD pipeline |
| 3 | Deploy to staging before production -- no direct-to-production deployments | Catch environment-specific issues before they affect users | Pipeline configuration |
| 4 | Deployments are automated -- no manual steps in the deployment pipeline | Manual steps introduce human error | Pipeline review |
| 5 | Track what was deployed, when, and by whom | Audit trail for incident investigation | Deployment log |

#### SHOULD (Recommended)

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Use progressive rollout (canary or blue-green) for production deployments | Limits blast radius of bad deployments | High-traffic services |
| 2 | Monitor error rates and latency during and after deployment | Catch deployment-related regressions immediately | All deployments |
| 3 | Avoid deploying on Fridays or before holidays | Reduces risk of weekend incidents | Unless the team has strong on-call coverage |
| 4 | Implement feature flags for large changes | Decouple deployment from release | Major features |

#### MAY (Optional Best Practices)

| # | Standard | Benefit | Trade-off |
|---|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Implement automatic rollback on error rate spike post-deploy | Fastest possible rollback | Requires reliable error rate baseline |
| 2 | Use GitOps for infrastructure and deployment configuration | Auditable, reproducible deployments | Tooling investment |

### Disaster Recovery

#### MUST (Non-Negotiable)

| # | Standard | Rationale | Verification |
|---|----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Define RPO (Recovery Point Objective) and RTO (Recovery Time Objective) for all critical services | Sets expectations for data loss and downtime tolerance | DR plan document |
| 2 | Automate backups for all critical data stores | Manual backups are unreliable | Backup monitoring |
| 3 | Test backup restoration at least quarterly | Untested backups are not backups | Restoration test log |

| # | Standard | Rationale | When to Apply |
|---|----------|-----------|---------------|

| 2 | Maintain infrastructure in multiple availability zones or regions | Survives single-zone failures | Production services |
| 3 | Store backups in a different region/account than production | Survives regional failures and account compromise | All backups |
| 4 | Automate failover where possible | Reduces human error during high-stress events | Critical services |
